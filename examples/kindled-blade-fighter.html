Fighter subclass · D&D 2024 · Example v1

KindledBlade

Example homebrew · shipped with Toon Anvil

"Steel remembers the forge. Mine never cooled."

Some smiths quench a blade in oil, some in blood. Your teacher quenched yours in nothing at all, and it has been giving the heat back ever since. The weapon is warm to the touch on the coldest morning, and warmer still when you have decided that something needs to end.

Kindled Blades fight patiently. The heat builds while you work, and the fighters who last are the ones who know when to let it out.

Features

Level 3

Ember Temper

Your weapon holds a working heat that answers to you.

KINDLED EDGE. Your weapon attacks deal an extra 1d6 Fire damage on a hit.

FORGE-WARMED. You have Resistance to Cold damage, and you can heat or cool a held object to a comfortable temperature at will.

BANKED HEAT. You gain a pool of Heat equal to your Proficiency Bonus. You regain all spent Heat when you finish a Short or Long Rest.

Level 7

Draw the Heat

As a Bonus Action, spend 1 Heat to pull the warmth out of the air around a creature you can see within 30 feet. It makes a Constitution saving throw against your Fighter save DC.

  • Failed save: it takes 2d8 Cold damage and its Speed is reduced by 10 feet until the end of its next turn.
  • Successful save: half damage, no reduction.

STOKED. Whenever a creature fails this save, your next weapon attack before the end of your next turn scores a critical hit on a roll of 19 or 20.

Level 10

Anneal

You have learned to spend the heat on yourself instead.

As a Bonus Action, spend 1 Heat to gain Temporary Hit Points equal to 2d6 + your Fighter level. While you have these, you have Advantage on saving throws against being Frightened.

Level 15

Quench

When a creature you can see within 5 feet hits you with a melee attack, you can use your Reaction and spend 1 Heat to release everything at once. The attacker takes 3d8 Fire damage and must succeed on a Constitution saving throw or be unable to take Reactions until the end of its next turn.

Level 18

The Blade Remembers

As a Magic action, you let the forge finish its work. For 1 minute:

  • Your weapon attacks deal an extra 2d6 Fire damage instead of 1d6.
  • You are immune to Fire and Cold damage.
  • Each creature that ends its turn within 10 feet of you takes 1d10 Fire damage.

Once you use this feature, you can't use it again until you finish a Long Rest.

Signs of the forge (d8)

Flavour. Roll one when you gain a subclass feature, or pick the one that suits the character.

1Frost never settles on your gear, however cold the night.
2Your breath doesn't fog. People notice in winter.
3Snow melts in a neat circle where you stand still.
4You sleep without a bedroll and wake comfortable.
5Candles gutter toward you as you pass.
6Your palms carry a permanent faint smell of hot iron.
7You can tell, without touching, whether a forge is lit.
8Your weapon hums quietly before a fight you will lose.

Design notes

This example is deliberately a little hot

Kindled Edge is an unconditional flat damage rider from level 3, which is the strongest kind of feature to hand a Fighter: it scales with Extra Attack and asks nothing in return. It is written this way on purpose so that Toon Anvil's balance verdict has something real to report, and so you can watch auto-balance propose a change and decide whether you agree.

Open questions worth taking to a DM: is Banked Heat too generous on a short rest? Does Stoked stack awkwardly with Champion's crit range in a multiclass?
